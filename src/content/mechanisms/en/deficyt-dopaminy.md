A neurotypical person 'inherently knows' the reward comes after the finished task, so tolerates the stress along the way. The ADHD brain discounts future reward — it needs it now. The same explains susceptibility to easy dopamine hits. Neuroscientist TJ Power distinguishes 'effort' dopamine (arriving after a hard, completed task — evolutionarily, after the hunt) from 'non-effort' dopamine (sugar, fast food, scrolling — grabbed without work), which dysregulates the reward system. The practical upshot: make the reward explicit and near-term, and bias toward effort dopamine over easy hits.
