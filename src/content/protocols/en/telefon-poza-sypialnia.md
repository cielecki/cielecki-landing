The strongest evidence on pre-sleep screens isn't about blue light but a simple fact: people who bring a phone to bed lose on average about 40 minutes to scrolling that could have been sleep. Time, not retinal chemistry, is the main culprit here — so the fix isn't a night filter, it's physically removing the phone from reach.

Concretely: put the charger outside the bedroom (the bathroom or living room) and charge the phone there overnight. Out of sight, out of mind — in ADHD that's literal. Since the charger lives there, you'll set the phone back in the same spot the next night, so the habit reinforces itself.

The bonus is the morning and your whole sleep rhythm: with the phone in another room it's harder to hit snooze — you have to get up to silence it. That stabilizes wake time, and a consistent wake time syncs circadian rhythm with sleep pressure, the two systems good sleep depends on.
