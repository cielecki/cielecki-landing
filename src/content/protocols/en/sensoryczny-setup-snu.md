Many people with ADHD also have sensory sensitivity, and sleep is an environment saturated with tactile and taste input. A scratchy blanket, a PJ tag, weird-tasting toothpaste, or a too-tight waistband can make the bedtime routine and falling asleep unpleasant enough that the brain starts avoiding bed — which pushes bedtime even later.

Approach it like engineering: walk through each step of the evening routine and catch the friction points. Cut tags, swap scratchy bedding for smooth, find a tolerable-tasting toothpaste, pick PJs without pressing seams. The goal is a sleep setup that feels good sensorily — then going to bed becomes something you want, not something you push through.

This isn't cosmetic, it's lowering the barrier to entry. In ADHD motivation follows comfort and low friction; by removing the sensory aversion you turn the bed from a place you avoid into one you're drawn to.
